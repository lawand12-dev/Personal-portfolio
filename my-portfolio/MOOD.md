# Phase 2 — Inspiration and Visual Direction

## Direction under consideration

**Terminal Editorial**: a dark, greyscale portfolio that pairs developer-tool cues with editorial typography and clear, human writing. Use terminal details as a visual language, not as a navigation puzzle. The projects and contact information should stay easy to find.

**Status:** Confirmed by Lawand for the next phase.

## Scene and job

- **Scene:** A recruiter or hiring manager opens Lawand's portfolio between other candidate tabs, scans it on a laptop or phone, and decides whether his frontend skills and projects merit a conversation.
- **Job:** Help a hiring manager quickly understand who Lawand is, review his work, and find a way to contact him.
- **Register:** Brand — this is a first-impression portfolio, not a recurring task interface.

## What to carry into the portfolio

- A clear role statement and name should take priority over decorative terminal details.
- Use monospaced type for short labels, code-like details, and metadata; keep longer biography and project descriptions comfortable to read.
- Use a restrained near-black and greyscale palette, with hierarchy created through type, spacing, and subtle surface and border differences.
- Keep project information and links conventional and easy to scan. A visitor should not need to understand a terminal command to browse the work.
- Let editorial references inform pacing and typography, while the developer portfolio remains direct and recruiter-friendly.
- Keep motion subordinate to the content; the project brief calls for reduced-motion support.

## Direction controls

These are working dials for the proposed direction, not implementation tokens:

- **Visual variance: 4/10** — distinctive through terminal/editorial structure, while staying close to the brief's strict greyscale system.
- **Motion intensity: 4/10** — restrained feedback and a small number of purposeful reveals; honor reduced-motion preferences.
- **Information density: 4/10** — leave room to read, but keep projects and contact details within easy reach.

## Guardrails

- No neon, gradients, glassmorphism, or colorful accent by default; the brief explicitly calls for strict greyscale.
- Avoid turning the whole site into a mock terminal. Use ordinary navigation, links, and readable content.
- Avoid generic portfolio filler and invented project details. The project ideas remain undecided and are intentionally not included here.
- Do not lock implementation-level tokens in this phase. The project brief reserves the detailed design system for Phase 5.

## Phase 2 review

**Decision:** Lawand confirmed **Terminal Editorial** as the direction to carry into Phase 3.
